Complete Guide: How Worldwide Time Zones Work

Earth rotates 360 degrees on its rotational axis every 24 hours, meaning it travels approximately 15 degrees of longitude per hour. In 1884, the International Meridian Conference established Greenwich, London as the Prime Meridian (0° Longitude), creating the foundation for the world's 24 standard 15-degree longitudinal time zones.

UTC vs. GMT: Understanding the Difference

While people often use Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) and Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) interchangeably, they have distinct technical definitions:

  • GMT (Greenwich Mean Time): A solar time zone based on Earth's rotation and the mean solar time at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London. The UK switches from GMT (UTC+0) to British Summer Time (BST, UTC+1) during daylight saving months.
  • UTC (Coordinated Universal Time): The official international time standard measured by high-precision atomic clocks (International Atomic Time) combined with leap seconds. UTC never changes with seasons or daylight saving time.

Daylight Saving Time (DST)

Approximately 70 countries observe Daylight Saving Time (DST), advancing clocks forward by 1 hour in Spring ("spring forward") and setting them back 1 hour in Autumn ("fall back"). For example, US Eastern Time shifts from Eastern Standard Time (EST, UTC-5) in winter to Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC-4) in summer. Countries near the equator (such as Singapore, Kenya, and Indonesia) and Asian nations (such as Pakistan, India, China, and Japan) do not observe DST, maintaining steady year-round offsets.

Non-Hourly Offsets: The Half-Hour & 45-Minute Zones

Several sovereign nations chose fractional UTC offsets to better center daylight across their territories:

  • India & Sri Lanka: Indian Standard Time (IST) is UTC+05:30.
  • Nepal: Nepal Time (NPT) is UTC+05:45, aligned with the meridian of Mount Everest.
  • Iran: Iran Standard Time (IRST) is UTC+03:30.
  • Central Australia: Australian Central Standard Time (ACST) is UTC+09:30 (or UTC+10:30 during AEDT).
  • Newfoundland (Canada): Newfoundland Standard Time (NST) is UTC-03:30.

The International Date Line (IDL)

The International Date Line runs along the 180° meridian in the Pacific Ocean. Crossing the line westward (e.g. flying from California to Tokyo or Sydney) advances the calendar by one full day (+1 day ahead). Crossing eastward moves the calendar backward by one day (-1 day behind). Our converter automatically flags cross-date conversions so you never miss flights, deadlines, or international webinars.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I convert time between two countries or time zones? ▾
Select your origin location in the "From" selector, enter your source date and time, and choose the target destination in the "To" selector. The tool dynamically computes the converted time, local date, hour difference, and business hour overlap.
What is the difference between UTC and GMT? ▾
Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is a historic astronomical time zone based on Greenwich, London. Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) is the modern atomic timekeeping standard. Both share the exact same current second and hour with zero offset (+00:00), but UTC is static and never adopts daylight saving time.
How does Daylight Saving Time (DST) affect my time conversion? ▾
DST advances local clocks by one hour during warmer months. In North America and Europe, DST alters the relative time difference to non-DST nations (such as Pakistan or India) by 1 hour twice a year. Our tool automatically references exact IANA seasonal daylight rules.
Why are some countries 30 or 45 minutes offset from UTC? ▾
Countries such as India (UTC+5:30), Nepal (UTC+5:45), and parts of Australia (UTC+9:30) chose intermediate offsets so noon on the clock matches solar noon across the central longitudinal width of their nation.
How does the International Date Line affect converted calendar days? ▾
When calculating time across the 180° meridian between the Western Hemisphere (e.g. USA) and the Eastern Hemisphere (e.g. Australia or Japan), local dates differ by 1 full day. The badge on our result box will clearly display "+1 day ahead (Tomorrow)" or "-1 day behind (Yesterday)".
What is the best way to schedule international meetings across global time zones? ▾
Utilize the 24-hour visual meeting planner above. Look for columns where both locations show green blocks (9:00 AM to 5:00 PM). For instance, connecting London (BST) with Pakistan (PKT) works seamlessly between 10:00 AM – 1:00 PM UK time, corresponding to 3:00 PM – 6:00 PM in Pakistan.
