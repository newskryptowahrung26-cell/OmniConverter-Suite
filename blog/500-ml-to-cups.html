500 mL to Cups: How Many Cups is 500 mL?

✍️ Author: Chef Claire Hastings 🔬 Reviewed by: Sarah Jenkins, MSc 📅 Last Reviewed: October 2026 ⏱️ 4 min read

Converting liquid volume between metric milliliters (mL) and traditional measuring cups is a fundamental kitchen task when preparing soups, baking cakes, or scaling international recipes. A frequent culinary query is: 500 mL is how many cups?

Quick Summary Answer

500 mL = 2.11 US Customary Cups (approx. 2 1/8 cups)
500 mL = 2.00 Metric Cups (exactly 2 cups in UK/AU/CA)

In Fluid Ounces: 16.91 US fl oz
In Pints: 1.06 US Pints (1.0567 pt)
In Liters: 0.5 Liters (Half a Liter)

🇬🇧 UK vs 🇺🇸 US Measurement Rule

• In the UK, Australia, New Zealand & Canada (Metric Cup = 250 mL): 500 mL is exactly 2 cups.
• In the United States (Customary Cup = 236.588 mL): 500 mL is 2.11 cups (approximately 2 cups plus 2 tablespoons).
• Everyday Kitchen Reference: 500 mL is exactly 0.5 Liter (half a liter), equal to 16.9 US fluid ounces (the standard single-serve water bottle) or 33.8 US tablespoons.

Volumetric vs Mass Measuring in Modern Culinary Arts

While measuring 500 mL in volume is straightforward with a calibrated liquid measuring pitcher, converting volume to mass in grams depends on ingredient bulk density. For example, 500 mL of pure water weighs exactly 500 grams (1.00 g/mL density).

However, 500 mL of granulated sugar weighs approximately 425 grams, whereas 500 mL of all-purpose flour weighs roughly 260 grams. Professional pastry chefs strongly advise using digital gram scales for dry ingredients while reserving liquid measuring cups for pure liquids like milk, water, broth, and oil.
