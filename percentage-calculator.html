Four Essential Percentage Formulas Explained

Percentages represent proportions expressed as fractions of 100. Whether analyzing personal investments, business profit margins, shopping sales discounts, or academic test scores, mastering the standard mathematical formulas ensures complete financial and analytical accuracy.

1. Finding the Percentage of a Given Number

To determine what a specific percentage of a whole amount is, convert the percentage into a decimal (divide by 100) and multiply by the total: P = (X × Y) ÷ 100. For example, 15% of $80 is (15 × 80) ÷ 100 = $12.

2. Finding What Percentage X is of Y

To find the relative proportion one number bears to another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100: P = (X ÷ Y) × 100. If a student scores 25 marks on a 200-mark examination, their percentage is (25 ÷ 200) × 100 = 12.5%.

3. Percentage Increase and Decrease

Measuring growth or decline between two values requires computing the change relative to the initial value: Change % = [((Final - Initial) ÷ |Initial|) × 100]. An asset moving from $100 down to $75 experiences a 25% decrease.

4. Reverse Percentage (Finding Original Pre-Discount Value)

When an item is discounted by D%, the sale price represents (100 - D)% of the original price. The original cost is calculated as: Original = Sale Price ÷ (1 - D/100). An item purchased for $80 after a 20% sale had an original price of $80 ÷ 0.80 = $100.

Percentage vs. Percentage Points: Avoiding Common Errors

A frequent point of confusion in financial reporting is the distinction between percent and percentage points. Percentage points measure the direct arithmetic difference between two percentage numbers. For instance, if an interest rate climbs from 5% to 6%, it has increased by 1 percentage point, but by 20% in relative terms (since 1 is 20% of 5).

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate what percentage one number is of another?

Divide the part by the total whole and multiply by 100. For example, to find what percentage 25 is of 200: (25 ÷ 200) × 100 = 12.5%.

What is the formula for percentage increase or decrease?

The formula is: [((New Value - Original Value) ÷ |Original Value|) × 100]. A positive result indicates percentage growth, while a negative result represents a percentage decrease.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A percentage represents a relative proportional change, while percentage points measure the direct arithmetic difference between two percentage rates.
