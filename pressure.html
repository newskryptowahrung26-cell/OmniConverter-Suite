Pressure Unit Conversion Guide

Pressure is force applied per unit area. The SI unit is the Pascal (Pa). Across automotive engineering, weather forecasting, scuba diving, medicine, hydraulics, and industrial manufacturing, converting between bar, psi, kPa, atm, and mmHg is essential for safety and precision.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q: How do I convert bar to psi?
A: Multiply bar by 14.5038. Example: 2.5 bar (typical car tyre) = 36.26 psi.

Q: What is 1 atm in psi?
A: 1 standard atmosphere = 14.6959 psi. Atmospheric pressure at sea level is defined as exactly 101,325 Pa.

Q: What pressure unit is used for tyres in the UK?
A: The UK uses psi (pounds per square inch) for tyre pressure. European countries often use bar. Typical car tyres run between 30–35 psi (2.0–2.4 bar).

Q: What is mmHg used for?
A: Millimetres of Mercury (mmHg) is used primarily for blood pressure readings and barometric atmospheric pressure. Normal blood pressure is 120/80 mmHg. Standard atmospheric pressure is 760 mmHg.

Q: How do I convert kPa to psi?
A: Divide kPa by 6.89476. Example: 200 kPa = 29.008 psi.

Practical Applications, Standards & FAQs

Pressure measurement is governed by ISO 80000-4 and NIST standards. Understanding the correct unit for each industry prevents safety errors in automotive, medical, industrial, and meteorological contexts.

Why do different industries use different pressure units?
The automotive industry in the UK and USA uses psi; European industry uses bar; science and meteorology use Pa and hPa; medicine uses mmHg. Each unit evolved from practical measuring instruments in those domains.

What is gauge pressure vs. absolute pressure?
Absolute pressure is measured relative to a perfect vacuum. Gauge pressure is measured relative to atmospheric pressure. Tyre pressure is typically gauge pressure — a "flat" tyre at 0 psi gauge still has 14.7 psi absolute.
